# Video 2, Version 2: "13 Minutes" (Arohak × Quanfluence)

**Idea:** tell it as a human story, not a data table. One emergency, one clock, one number people remember: **13 minutes**.
**Length:** about 60 seconds, 1080p, loops. Short enough for people walking past the stall.
**Style:** dark, cinematic, big text, one idea per screen, very few words. Works fully on mute.
**Accuracy:** a single badge, **"Simulation on real 108 call data"**, stays on every results screen, so captions don't have to repeat "simulated" in every line.

## What's different from Version 1

| Version 1 | Version 2 |
|---|---|
| A data comparison (split screen, 4 milestones, tables) | A story: one call, one family, one number |
| About 80 seconds | About 60 seconds |
| "Simulated" in every caption | One clear badge plus a footnote, so captions stay clean |
| Several numbers per screen | One number per screen; **13 minutes** is the hero |
| Starts with logos | Starts with emotion (2:14 AM call); logos at the end |

## The numbers used (unchanged)

| | Live 108 | QUBO (simulated) |
|---|---|---|
| Call → patient | 22.6 min | 9.8 min (**13 min sooner**) |
| Call → hospital care (QUBO incl. +15 at scene, +15 handover) | 60.6 min | 47.5 min (**13 min sooner**) |

## Accuracy notes

- The 2:14 AM call and "my father has collapsed" are an **illustrative scenario**, and the footnote says so. All the minutes are the real averages.
- "13 minutes can change everything" is a general statement. It doesn't claim a specific medical outcome.
- The 108 times are live data; the QUBO times are simulated. That's stated on the badge, in the footnote and in the roadmap ("Tested on real 108 data. Next: a live trial").
